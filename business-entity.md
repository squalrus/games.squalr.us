# Business Entity: LLC vs. Individual

**Recommendation: form an LLC before applying to Nintendo; it's optional-but-cheap-insurance for Android/Xbox/Steam alone.**

| Factor | Individual / Sole Proprietor | LLC |
|---|---|---|
| Liability | Personal assets exposed if sued (e.g., a copyright/IP claim from in-game assets, a payment dispute) | Liability generally limited to LLC assets |
| Google Play Console | Individual account type supported, $25 one-time fee | Organization account type supported, same fee |
| Microsoft Partner Center | Individual account supported (free) | Company account supported (also free registration, costs nothing extra over individual) |
| Steamworks | Individual account supported, $100 one-time fee per app | Company account supported, same fee — Valve has no entity-type gating like Nintendo |
| Nintendo Developer Portal | Historically required a registered company; Nintendo has a more limited self-publish path for individuals in some regions but vetting is stricter and slower than for a registered business | The standard, well-trodden path — most indie Switch ports go through an LLC or equivalent |
| Taxes | Business income is personal income (self-employment tax) | Default pass-through taxation (same as sole prop) unless you elect S-corp; can deduct business expenses more cleanly |
| Banking/payouts | Can technically receive Play/Xbox/Steam payouts as an individual | Cleaner to have a business bank account tied to an EIN, separate from personal accounts — all four platforms pay out to whatever entity is on file |
| Cost/effort | $0, no filing | State filing fee (commonly $50–$500 depending on state) + annual report/franchise tax in many states + need an EIN (free, IRS) |

Given the plan is to pursue **all four platforms**, including Switch where Nintendo's bar for individuals is highest, forming an LLC now avoids re-doing account paperwork later and gives you liability separation once real revenue and a published storefront listing exist. This is a decision only you can finalize — pick your state, name, and registered agent — but the direction (LLC) is the safer default here.
